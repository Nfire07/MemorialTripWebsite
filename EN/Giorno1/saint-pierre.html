Saint-Pierre-de-Montmartre

Overshadowed by the nearby Basilica, this is one of the oldest churches in Paris, a silent witness to centuries of history on the hill.

A Hidden Gem

The church of Saint-Pierre de Montmartre is all that remains of the great Royal Abbey of Montmartre, founded in the 12th century by King Louis VI. Consecrated in 1147 by Pope Eugene III, it is considered one of the oldest churches in Paris, along with Saint-Germain-des-Prés.

Unlike the lavish Basilica of the Sacred Heart, Saint-Pierre retains an intimate atmosphere, and its architecture speaks to us of the transition from Romanesque to early Gothic. Inside, you can admire four marble columns (presumably recovered from an ancient temple dedicated to Mars or Mercury) and the oldest cross vaults in Paris.

Don't Miss

The Roman Columns

Four columns with composite capitals at the entrance to the choir and in the nave, vestiges of an ancient pagan temple that stood on the hill.

The Calvaire Cemetery

Adjacent to the church is the smallest and oldest cemetery in Paris (open only on November 1st), where many aristocrats and historical figures linked to Montmartre rest.

The Bronze Doors

The modern entrance doors, work of sculptor Gismondi (1980), depict scenes from the life of Saint Peter and the history of Montmartre.
