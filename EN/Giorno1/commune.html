The Paris Commune (1871)

From the hill of Montmartre to the political heart of the city: 72 days of revolution that changed the history of Paris.

Monuments Itinerary Useful Info

Historical Context

After the Franco-Prussian War (1870) and the fall of Napoleon III’s Empire, Paris found itself occupied, starving and divided. On 18 March 1871, the government led by Adolphe Thiers attempted to seize the National Guard’s cannons on Montmartre hill, sparking an uprising: thus the Paris Commune was born, the first experiment in direct democracy in modern history. The Commune lasted only 72 days, but left behind a political and symbolic legacy that still fascinates and divides today.

Symbolic Places of the Commune

Three essential stops to understand the 1871 uprising

Montmartre

Considered the birthplace of the uprising. The hill was where the National Guard had positioned its cannons. The government’s attempt, led by Adolphe Thiers, to seize them on 18 March 1871 failed, triggering the revolt that gave rise to the Commune.

Hôtel de Ville (City Hall)

The historic building in Place de Grève (today Place de l’Hôtel-de-Ville) was the nerve centre of the Commune: the revolutionary council was installed here, and the red flag flew above it. During the Bloody Week (May 1871) it was burnt down by government troops and later rebuilt identically.

Tuileries Palace

This royal and imperial residence symbolised monarchical power and that of Napoleon III. The Communards burned it down on 23 May 1871 to symbolise the end of the previous regime. Today, the Jardin des Tuileries, one of Paris’s most famous parks, occupies its place.

Educational Route

Montmartre → Tuileries Palace → Hôtel de Ville

  • Total Duration: about 1 hour and a half.
  • Objective: understanding the birth, government and fall of the Commune.
  • Focus: the relationship between political power and urban symbols.

Practical Information

  • Access: metro Abbesses (Montmartre), Hôtel de Ville, Tuileries (line 1).
  • Behaviour: respect public spaces and parks.
  • Materials: maps, summary sheet and historical images.
