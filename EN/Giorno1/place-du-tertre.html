Place du Tertre

The beating heart of Montmartre, where painters, portrait artists, and visitors keep the neighborhood's bohemian tradition alive.

History and Atmosphere

Place du Tertre is located just a few steps from the Basilica of the Sacred Heart and is the center of the old village of Montmartre. At the end of the 18th century, it was the main square of the independent village. Today it is famous worldwide for its street artists: every day, dozens of painters and caricaturists set up their easels, continuing the tradition that saw masters like Picasso, Utrillo, and Toulouse-Lautrec live here at the beginning of the 20th century.

Despite the influx of tourists, the square retains its historical charm, surrounded by small restaurants and bistros housed in 18th-century buildings. It is the ideal place to immerse yourself in the artistic atmosphere of Paris and watch life go by.

Curiosities

The Artists

To work in the square, artists must obtain an official concession from the City of Paris. Space is highly contested, and the waiting list is years long.

The First "Bistro"

It is said that the word "bistro" was born right here in 1814, when occupying Russian soldiers demanded drinks "bystro" (quickly) from local restaurateurs.

The Virtual Guillotine

Not far from the square, at number 6 rue des Abbesses, you can see the church of Saint-Jean de Montmartre, but historically Place du Tertre was not a place of execution, contrary to popular legends.
