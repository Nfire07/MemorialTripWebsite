On the Eiffel Tower: A Journey on "La Dame de Fer" (The Iron Lady)

The Eiffel Tower, built by Gustave Eiffel between 1887 and 1889 for the Universal Exhibition, stands 330 meters tall and is one of the most famous monuments in the world. Initially criticized, it became a symbol of technical innovation and modernity. The tower has three visitable levels, was saved thanks to its use as a radio antenna, and today welcomes over 7 million visitors a year, becoming a cultural and tourist icon of Paris.

Recommended duration: 3 hours • Access with booked ticket • Free photography

Main stages

Stage 1

Mars Field

The Champs de Mars is the large green space at the foot of the Eiffel Tower, extending to the Military School. Originally, in the 18th century, it was a military training ground used for exercises and parades by French soldiers. Over time, it has become a public and symbolic space, the setting for festivals, national celebrations, and historical protests. Today, it is one of the most famous parks in Paris, perfect for picnics, photos, and for enjoying the view of the Eiffel Tower, especially at sunset.

Stage 2

Office on the third floor

At the top of the Eiffel Tower, approximately 276 meters above sea level, lies a small private room that belonged to Gustave Eiffel. The engineer used it as an office and laboratory for scientific experiments and meetings with important figures of the time, including Thomas Edison. Today, it has been reconstructed with lifelike mannequins representing Eiffel, Edison, and other guests, to show visitors how it looked back then. It's a historic corner, suspended in the clouds, revealing the most personal and visionary side of the tower's creator.
