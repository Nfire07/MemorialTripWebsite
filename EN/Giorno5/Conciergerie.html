Inside the Concengerie: From Prison to Palace of Justice

The Conciergerie, on the Île de la Cité in Paris, was originally the residence of the kings of France until the 14th century, when the court moved to the Louvre and Vincennes. It then became the seat of the Parlement of Paris and a royal prison, administered by the concierge, from whom it took its name. During the French Revolution, it was the main prison of the Revolutionary Tribunal, housing Marie Antoinette, among others. It remained a prison until the 19th century, when it was transformed into a historical monument and museum, a symbol of French history between monarchy and revolution.

Recommended duration: 2 hours • Access with booked ticket • Photography without flash

Main stages

Stage 1

Clock

The Conciergerie clock, installed in 1371, is considered the oldest public clock in Paris. It is located on the Tour de l'Horloge, the clock tower overlooking the Seine. Crafted by master clockmaker Henri de Vic, it served to keep time for all Parisians, when personal watches were still a luxury. It has been restored several times over the centuries, and its gilded dial with blue decorations and royal fleurs-de-lis remains a symbol of monarchical power and ancient French technical precision.

Stage 2

Marie Antoinette's Cell

During the French Revolution, Queen Marie Antoinette was imprisoned in the Conciergerie in 1793, awaiting trial and the guillotine. Her cell was small, cold, and under constant guard. Today, the site has been transformed into a memorial chapel called the "Chapelle expiatoire," which houses objects and reconstructions commemorating the queen's final days. It is one of the most visited and moving spaces in the entire Conciergerie, a symbol of the collapse of the French monarchy.
