Inside Versailles: a journey through the royal interiors

From the Hall of Mirrors to the King’s and Queen’s apartments, including the state rooms and private chambers: this page guides the exploration of Versailles’ most emblematic interiors, with reflections on symbolism, art, and court life.

Main stops

Stop 1

Hall of Mirrors (Galerie des Glaces)

The most famous room in Versailles: 357 mirrors reflect the windows overlooking the gardens. The ceiling is decorated with frescoes depicting the achievements of Louis XIV and the glory of absolutism. This room is a symbolic and choreographed element of royal representation.

Stop 2

King’s Apartments

These private and official rooms were used by the monarch for ceremonies, audiences, and daily life. Each room is decorated with styles and allegories referring to cosmic order, royalty, and power. It is interesting to observe the stylistic evolution between rooms.

Stop 3

State and ceremonial rooms

Rooms such as the Throne Room, audience halls, and side galleries were designed to impress guests and receive ambassadors. Precious materials, high ceilings, and gilded ornaments reflect the relationship between art and power in the absolute monarchy.

Stop 4

The Royal Chapel

The last part of the palace to be completed, the chapel was consecrated in 1710. The frescoes illustrate religious and monarchical themes, linking sacred and royal power, reflecting the ideal of the king as a spiritually invested figure.
