Musée Carnavalet — History of Paris

In the vibrant heart of the picturesque Marais district, the Musée Carnavalet is a true treasure chest of Parisian memory. Entirely dedicated to the city’s history, this unique museum preserves over 600,000 objects that recount the evolution of Paris from its Gallo-Roman origins to the contemporary era. Every room, every detail, every document is a window into different epochs, revolutions, urban transformations, and the daily lives that shaped the identity of the French capital.

A museum in the heart of the Marais

The museum is housed in two magnificent historic residences: Hôtel Carnavalet and Hôtel Le Peletier de Saint-Fargeau, masterpieces of Parisian Renaissance and Baroque architecture. Hôtel Carnavalet, in particular, was the home of the famous letter-writer Madame de Sévigné, whose correspondence offers a lively and ironic view of 17th-century aristocratic society.

Inaugurated in 1880, the Carnavalet is the oldest municipal museum in Paris. After an important four-year renovation, it reopened in 2021 with a renewed exhibition path and free access. Its rooms evoke atmospheres of the past: reconstructions of historical interiors, original shop signs, paintings, photographs, and archival documents intertwine to create a visual and sensory narrative of the city.

The Marais district, where the museum stands, is one of the oldest and most fascinating areas of Paris. Once a marshland — marais literally means “swamp” — it was drained and transformed in the 17th century into an elegant residential neighborhood for the nobility. Today it retains a unique character, with hidden courtyards, secret gardens, cobblestone streets, and a lively cultural scene featuring art galleries, independent bookshops, and historic cafés. Walking through the Marais feels like leafing through an open-air history book.

Why it matters

Visiting the Musée Carnavalet means embarking on a journey through two thousand years of Parisian history: from the founding of Lutetia to the medieval era, from the French Revolution to the urban transformations of Haussmann, all the way to the social and cultural struggles of the 20th century. The museum’s collections offer an immersive and interdisciplinary experience, ideal for students and enthusiasts of history, art, and literature.

Among the most striking sections are:

  • The 18th-century salons, with Louis XV–style furnishings and spectacular trompe-l’œil decoration.
  • The trade signs, testimony to Parisian commercial life.
  • The revolutionary rooms, with authentic relics, portraits of key figures, and republican symbols.
Each room is designed to evoke historical atmospheres and stimulate reflection on the social and cultural changes that shaped the city.

What to observe (class focus)

  • Revolutions section: flags, medals, busts, and posters illustrating the birth of the modern citizen. Analyze the evolution of republican symbolism: liberté, égalité, fraternité.
  • Interior reconstructions: from Baroque opulence to 19th-century bourgeois salons. Compare styles, materials, and the social functions of spaces.
  • Toponymy and maps: explore Paris’s urban transformations, Haussmann’s redesign, the creation of the grands boulevards, and the metamorphosis of the Marais from aristocratic neighborhood to working-class area and finally cultural district.

Practical information

  • Address: 23 Rue de Sévigné, 75003 (Marais)
  • Entry: permanent collections generally free
  • Opening hours: 10:00–18:00 (closed Monday) — check the official website
  • Transport: M1 Saint-Paul, M8 Chemin Vert, bus lines 69/96
  • Accessibility: accessible routes and elevators; priority access for school groups by reservation

Curiosities and insights

Did you know? One of the museum’s most famous rooms preserves the key to the Bastille, a gift from La Fayette to George Washington as a symbol of liberty. Many of the signs on display — such as those of the “Chat Noir” or the “Belle Jardinière” — come from shops that truly existed in historic Paris.
