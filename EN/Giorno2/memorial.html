Mémorial de la Shoah — Paris

The Mémorial de la Shoah in Paris, located in the heart of the Marais district, is a place of remembrance dedicated to the victims of the Holocaust. Inaugurated in 2005, it collects documents, photographs, and testimonies that tell the story of the persecutions suffered by Jews during the Second World War. Among its main spaces are the crypt, the Center for Contemporary Jewish Documentation, and the Wall of the Righteous—symbols of memory, reflection, and commitment against all forms of discrimination.

Historical Context and Neighborhood

The Mémorial de la Shoah in Paris is a fundamental site for understanding the history of the Holocaust in France. Located at 17 rue Geoffroy-l'Asnier, in the heart of the Marais district in the 4th arrondissement, the memorial stands in an area that, prior to World War II, was home to a large Jewish community. This historic neighborhood witnessed the persecutions and deportations suffered by French Jews during the Nazi occupation.

Inaugurated on January 27, 2005, coinciding with Holocaust Remembrance Day and the 60th anniversary of the liberation of Auschwitz, the Mémorial de la Shoah is the primary center for Holocaust documentation and commemoration in France. It was established by the merger of the Centre de Documentation Juive Contemporaine and the Mémorial du Martyr Juif Inconnu, the latter founded in 1956 to pay tribute to the victims of the Shoah. The memorial houses a vast collection of documents, photographs, personal objects, and testimonies that tell the story of the 76,000 Jews deported from France between 1940 and 1944.

The Memorial was created to preserve the traces of broken lives and to offer tools for historical understanding: it is not just a museum, but a documentation center, an archive, and an educational laboratory open to schools and citizens.

Key Elements of the Visit

  • Wall of Names: Inaugurated on January 27, 2005, it lists the names of 75,568 Jews deported from France.
  • Crypt and Remembrance Spaces: The crypt is located beneath the parvis (the large entrance square) of the memorial. It is a place of silence and contemplation, designed as a symbolic tomb for the six million Jewish victims of the genocide who died without a burial.
  • Documentation Center: Archives, photographs, lists, and letters; an essential tool for reconstructing family histories and local contexts.
  • Exhibitions and Cultural Outreach: In addition to permanent displays, there are temporary exhibitions, conferences, and screenings.
  • Wall of the Righteous: Lists the names of the men and women honored as "Righteous Among the Nations" who risked their lives to save French Jews during the Second World War.

What to Observe (Class Focus)

  • The Language of Memory: Observe how the names of the victims are presented. What effect does the choice to avoid "spectacular" objects—privileging lists, documents, and testimonies—produce?
  • Documents and Biographies: Select a document (e.g., a photograph, an ID card, a list) and try to imagine the minimal biography behind it: age, frequented places, family or social relationships, work performed, denied rights.
  • Topography of the Marais: Identify traces of Jewish presence in the neighborhood, such as plaques, signs, or synagogues, and try to connect urban spaces to the historical memory they hold.

Practical Info

  • Address: 17 Rue Geoffroy-l’Asnier, 75004 (Marais)
  • Admission: Free (security checks at the entrance; backpacks/bags verified)
  • Hours: Approximately 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM — check the official website for any closures
  • Transportation: M7 Pont Marie · M1 Saint-Paul · Bus 67/69/96
  • Accessibility: Ramps and elevators; school groups by reservation. Entrance requires respectful behavior and a low voice.

Fun Facts and Insights

Did you know? In 2019, the Wall of Names was closed to the public for a complete revision. 6,200 corrections were made, including additions and deletions of names. On January 27, 2020, the wall was re-inaugurated in the presence of President Emmanuel Macron.
