Mémorial de la Shoah — Paris

The Shoah Memorial in Paris, in the heart of the Marais district, is a place of remembrance dedicated to the victims of the Holocaust. Inaugurated in 2005, it gathers documents, photographs, and testimonies that tell the story of the persecutions suffered by Jews during the Second World War. Among its main spaces are the crypt, the Contemporary Jewish Documentation Center, and the Wall of the Righteous, symbols of memory, reflection, and commitment against all forms of discrimination.

Historical context and district

The Shoah Memorial in Paris is a key site of memory for understanding the history of the Holocaust in France. Located at 17 rue Geoffroy-l'Asnier, in the heart of the Marais district in the 4th arrondissement, the memorial stands in an area that, before the Second World War, was home to a large Jewish community.

This historic district witnessed the persecutions and deportations suffered by French Jews during the Nazi occupation.

Inaugurated on 27 January 2005, to coincide with Holocaust Remembrance Day and the 60th anniversary of the liberation of Auschwitz, the Shoah Memorial is the main centre for documentation and commemoration of the Holocaust in France. It was created through the merger of the Centre de Documentation Juive Contemporaine and the Mémorial du Martyr Juif Inconnu, the latter founded in 1956 to honour the victims of the Shoah.

The memorial houses a vast collection of documents, photographs, personal belongings, and testimonies that tell the story of the 76,000 Jews deported from France between 1940 and 1944.

The Memorial was conceived to preserve the trace of lives cut short and to offer tools for historical understanding: not just a museum, but a documentation centre, an archive, and an educational laboratory open to schools and citizens.

Key elements of the visit

  • Wall of Names: inaugurated on 27 January 2005, it bears the list of the names of 75,568 Jews deported from France.
  • Crypt and spaces of remembrance: the crypt is located beneath the parvis (the large entrance square) of the memorial. It is a place of silence and contemplation, conceived as a symbolic tomb for the six million Jewish victims of the genocide, who died without burial.
  • Documentation centre: archives, photographs, lists, letters; an essential tool for reconstructing family histories and local contexts.
  • Exhibitions and cultural mediation: in addition to permanent displays, there are temporary exhibitions as well as conferences and film screenings.
  • Wall of the Righteous: bears the names of the men and women recognised as “Righteous” because they risked their lives to save French Jews during the Second World War.

What to observe (class focus)

  • The language of remembrance: Observe how the names of the victims are presented. What effect is produced by the choice not to use “spectacular” objects, but instead to focus on lists, documents, and testimonies?

  • Documents and biographies: Choose a document (for example a photograph, an ID card, a list) and try to imagine the minimal biography behind it: age, places frequented, family or social relationships, work carried out, rights denied.

  • Topography of the Marais: Identify traces of Jewish presence in the district, such as plaques, shop signs, or synagogues, and try to connect urban spaces with the historical memory they preserve.

Practical information

  • Address: 17 Rue Geoffroy-l’Asnier, 75004 (Marais)
  • Entry: free (security checks at the entrance; bags/backpacks inspected)
  • Opening hours: approximately 10:00–18:00 — check the official website for any closures
  • Transport: M7 Pont Marie · M1 Saint-Paul · Bus 67/69/96
  • Accessibility: ramps and lifts; school groups by reservation. Entrance requires respectful behaviour and a low speaking voice.

Curiosities and further insights

Did you know? In 2019 the Wall of Names was closed to the public for a complete review. A total of 6,200 corrections were made, including additions and removals of names. On 27 January 2020 the wall was inaugurated again in the presence of President Emmanuel Macron.
