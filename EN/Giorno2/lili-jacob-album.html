“An Auschwitz album” Exhibition

Also known as the “Lili Jacob’s Album”, this is a collection of photographs taken in 1944 on the arrival ramp of Auschwitz-Birkenau during the convoys, specifically from Hungary. They document with stark clarity the arrival, the selection, and the initial stages of dispossession. The album was found in 1945 by the young deportee Lili Jacob in a barrack of a camp where she had been transferred: she understood the historical value of these images and preserved them, allowing historians, courts, and witnesses to identify faces, locations, and procedures.

Historical Context

The photographs were taken by members of the SS photographic service at Birkenau during the spring–summer of 1944, during the mass deportation phase of Hungarian Jews. The sequences show arriving trains, prisoners in line, inspections, and the separation between those “fit for work” and those sent directly to the crematoria.

Lili Jacob's discovery in 1945 transformed these images into primary evidence: thanks to the album, many families were able to reconstruct paths and recognize people and contexts.

In the post-war period, copies and reproductions were used in trials and exhibitions, contributing to the public memory of the Holocaust. The controlled circulation of the album has also facilitated comparative studies of locations, uniforms, and procedures.

What We Learn

  • Photography as Evidence: Evidentiary value and limits (framing, point of view, absences).
  • Vocabulary of Deportation: Arrivals, selections, registrations, dispossession of goods, loss of name.
  • Sequence and Causal Links: Reading the photos as stages of an administrative-industrial process.

What to Observe (Class Focus)

  • Composition and Details: Signs, personal objects, gestures.
  • The Ramp as Space: Flow organization and roles.
  • Cause and Effect: Connecting images to regulations and convoy logistics.

Practical Info

  • Location: Mémorial de la Shoah (Exhibition space)
  • Admission: Free; security checks at the entrance
  • Visit Duration: 20–30 minutes
